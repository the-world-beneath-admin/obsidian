# 2026-05-16 TWB Trenchworks Production And Military System Consolidated Design

Autogenerated consolidation worker report.

## Scope

Standalone Unity 2D TWB Trenchworks prototype under The World Beneath umbrella.

This began as a design/documentation consolidation only. A later playtest stabilization pass appended live prototype scale and entry-zone notes here. No permanent memory, wiki, index, hot, or log files were edited.

## 1. Executive Summary

Trenchworks should become a factory-fed, team-based trench-war simulation. The player builds the industrial base, allocates contested goods between immediate war need, higher-tier reserve, and factory expansion, then spawns teams rather than individual soldiers. Individual units still exist under the hood for one-cell occupancy, combat, posture, scouting, supply use, and death.

The core game loop is:

```text
Resource nodes -> workers/machines -> goods -> allocation
  -> Front supplies, Reserve crafting, or Factory expansion
  -> military teams scout, fight, consume supplies, and dig in after contact
  -> emplacements and bombardment prepare enemy-base annihilation
```

The important correction is that the war should not be a moving front stripe. The visible front emerges from team positions, contact zones, trenches, fortifications, supply routes, emplacements, and base pressure. Trenches and defenses are contact-born: units do not build or dig in before enemy contact. Scouts may observe, crouch, hide, and mark suspicion, but fortifications emerge only after confirmed or held contact. Over time, isolated foxholes should connect into trench lines, trench lines should connect to nearby hardpoints, and communication/supply trenches should dig back toward base because the war discovers hot spots and difficult approaches.

### Current Prototype Map Scale And Entry Zones

- Live prototype map target: `500 x 500` factory grid and `1000 x 600` war grid.
- The war grid is understood as `1000` cells wide by `600` cells high so the left-to-right player/enemy pressure axis still has enough length.
- Each side has three `10 x 10` entry zones: top, middle, and bottom.
- The player-facing war lever is entry selection: future team spawn orders should choose top, middle, or bottom rather than asking the player to place individual units.
- The first live slice resets the current skirmish into the chosen lane and mirrors the enemy lane so playtests still reach contact. Later enemy AI can choose, weight, or split entry lanes.
- Entry lane selection should become a small strategic choice alongside troop/team composition: top for flank pressure, middle for shorter direct pressure, bottom for alternate supply and trench-network development.

## 2. Core Design Pillars

- Simulation first: production, transport, team behavior, supply, posture, and combat should run from inspectable rules.
- Teams, not unit spam: the player spawns and tracks teams. A team has a leader plus at least 3 sub-units.
- Individual unit truth: sub-units still occupy cells, scout, crouch, fire, carry goods, dig, man weapons, and suffer casualties.
- Supply-driven war: outcomes should remain mostly supply/state driven with bounded seeded variance.
- Contact-born fortification: no pre-contact trench maze. The battlefield discovers where fortifications matter.
- Three-way allocation: contested goods can route to Front, Reserve, or Factory.
- Steam as machine power: Coal feeds boilers, boilers generate steam, and an underground steam-pipe layer powers most industrial machines. Coal can still be a direct recipe input for smelting and chemistry, but boiler-fed steam replaces the normal factory-game "generator plus power cable" model.
- WW1 plus TWB twist: rifles, trenches, wire, artillery, coal, steam, food, labour, dark essence, wards, and steampunk-esque robotics.
- No trains: bulk transport should use cable skids, crawl-haulers, overhead chainways, pneumatic vault tubes, or aether-lift gantries.
- Readable diagnostics: if a team stalls, retreats, starves, refuses assault, or cannot dig, the UI should say why.

## 3. Production Economy Overview

The economy has five broad tiers:

- Tier 1, Hand War Economy: manual porters, labourers, hand benches, early rations, loose ammo, tools, sandbags, field dressings.
- Tier 2, Belted Workshop Line: conveyors begin here, with loaders, sorters, presses, canners, wire twisters, sawmills, and small boilers.
- Tier 3, Steam Yard And Chemical Works: steam engines, chemical vats, shell forges, concrete mixers, hospitals, signal benches, repair bays.
- Tier 4, Arcane Industrial Works: aether condensers, warded storage, command relays, automata support, bulk hauler docks, heavy shells.
- Tier 5, Siege And Binding Works: siege shells, bombardment cores, harmonic targeting, strategic reserves, war choir systems, final base-breach goods.

The production system must support three demand lanes at the same time:

- Frontline supply: food, ammo, medical, construction goods, command, shells, energy, replacement capacity.
- Upward crafting reserve: goods withheld to unlock stronger machines, teams, emplacements, siege systems, and final bombardment.
- Factory expansion: steel, machine parts, conveyors, boilers, foundations, worker buildings, pipes, gears, valves, essence conduits, and wards.

## 5. Worker System

Workers make the factory feel alive and create the first food pressure.

Worker types:

- Labourer: general manual work, loading, basic harvesting.
- Porter: Tier 1 item movement by walking and carrying.
- Farmer: operates farm machines.
- Machine Operator: runs benches, canners, looms, presses, and standard machines.
- Steam Engineer: runs boilers, engine houses, steam carts, pressure systems.
- Essence Tender: handles essence taps, farm infusion, condensers, wards, and magic machines.
- Mechanic: repairs belts, boilers, machines, loaders, and robotics.
- Quartermaster: prioritizes storage, food split, shipments, and depot flow.
- Factory Builder: constructs machines, belts, depots, infrastructure, and worker buildings.
- Clockwork Servitor: late robotic labour; consumes energy and repair, not food.

Assignments should happen by job category and zone rather than per-worker micromanagement. Job boards or depot priorities should cover Food, Mining, Hauling, Production, Repair, Construction, and War Shipment.

Food and fatigue:

- Human workers consume Food continuously.
- Heavy jobs consume more food and generate more fatigue.
- Mild hunger slows walking, hauling, farming, and construction.
- Moderate hunger causes workers to abandon low-priority tasks and increases accidents.
- Severe hunger stalls the factory and interrupts war shipments.
- Recovery should be gradual, not instant.

## 6. Food, Coal, And Essence Energy Systems

Food, Coal, and Essence are the three foundational sustaining resources.

Food:

- Feeds factory workers and military teams.
- Drives morale, fatigue recovery, scouting endurance, digging stamina, and replacement readiness.
- Must be allocated between Worker Food, Front Food, and Reserve.

Coal:

- Feeds boilers and furnaces.
- Boilers convert Coal plus Water into Steam.
- Steam moves through an underground steam-pipe layer and is the primary power source for most machines: canners, mixers, mills, powered loaders, presses, chemical vats, shell forges, steam carts, and later bulk logistics.
- Coal can still be consumed directly as a recipe input where it makes physical sense, such as Coal plus Iron/Scrap for smelting and steel.
- Coal or water shortages lower steam pressure and slow or stop connected machines.

Essence:

- Powers magic, growth, wards, aether devices, command relays, TWB units, and late siege stabilization.
- Required by farm machines, so the first magic conflict is food growth versus magical industry/war tools.
- Essence overdraw should cause instability: spoilage, machine stalls, worker sickness, misfires, or variance spikes.

Naming rule:

- Essence is the base magical resource.
- Aether Batteries are crafted storage/output devices made from Essence, copper, and stabilizers.

## 7. Farming And Essence-Fed Food Production

Food production begins with a farm machine, not raw grain magically becoming rations.

Core chain:

```text
Fungal Loam / Root-Meal Stock + Water + Essence + Farmer Labour
  -> Farm Machine
  -> Raw Food
  -> Worker Meals, Basic Rations, Meal Tins, Ration Crates, Strategic Ration Reserves
```

Farm tiers:

- Tier 1: Hand-Tended Mudbed Farm. Low yield, high labour, Essence required.
- Tier 2: Belt-Fed Grow Trough. Conveyor-friendly, better throughput.
- Tier 3: Steam-Heated Greenhouse. Coal/steam improves yield and stability.
- Tier 4: Warded Essence Hothouse. Higher yield, lower spoilage, warding cost.
- Tier 5: Strategic Ration Conservatory. Produces reserve food for offensives and siege.

Farm risks:

- No Essence: farm slows or stalls.
- Poor warding: Essence blight, spoiled food, worker sickness.
- Worker fatigue: lower yield.
- Over-feeding the front: factory workers slow down and may reduce total future food.

## 9. Conveyor, Machine, Steam, And Magic Infrastructure Chains

Conveyors start in Tier 2.

Basic conveyor:

```text
Fiber / Leather + Planks or Iron Frames + Rollers + Bearings + Hand Tools
  -> Conveyor Belt Segment
```

Powered conveyor:

```text
Conveyor Segment + Drive Shaft + Gearbox + Bearings + Steam Drive
  -> Powered Conveyor Line
```

Machine construction should use kits:

- Foundations: packed foundation, brick, or concrete foundation.
- Frames: crude frame, iron frame, reinforced frame.
- Machine Parts: simple parts, industrial parts, precision mechanisms.
- Boilers and steam pipes: boiler kit, underground steam-pipe segments, valve, gauge.
- Tools: hand tools, machine tools, advanced tooling.

Steam infrastructure:

```text
Coal + Water + Boiler Kit
  -> Boiler / Steam Engine House
  -> Steam
  -> Underground Steam Pipe Layer
  -> Powered Machines / Loaders / Presses / Canners / Forges / Mixers / Bulk Docks
```

Steam-pipe rule:

- Steam pipes live on an underground factory layer so the surface remains readable for belts, workers, machines, and roads.
- Machines that need power must connect to the steam network or sit in boiler adjacency range for the earliest prototype slice.
- Boilers are the power producers; steam pipes are the power distribution network.
- Steam pressure, leaks, valve quality, and boiler throughput become the industrial equivalent of power capacity.
- Coal remains allowed as a direct material input for recipes such as steel, coke, chemicals, and shell production, but the default machine-power model is boiler-to-steam-to-pipe.

Magic infrastructure:

```text
Essence + Copper + Bone-Salt + Steel
  -> Essence Conduits, Ward Plates, Stabilizers
  -> farms, condensers, relays, warded storage, searchlights, siege support
```

No-train bulk transport:

- Tier 4 Cable-Haul Freight Skids: dock-to-dock armored pallets along cable routes.
- Tier 4 Crawl-Haulers: slow walking freight platforms for large batches.
- Tier 4 Overhead Chainways: suspended crate carriers over factory traffic.
- Tier 5 Pneumatic Vault Tubes: sealed hub-to-hub batch transport for crates/canisters/shells.
- Tier 5 Aether-Lift Gantries: expensive short-hop heavy transfer between anchor pads.

## 11. Military Team System

The player spawns teams, not individual units.

Team record:

- Team id, faction, tier, tasking family, template.
- Leader unit type plus at least 3 sub-units.
- Current order, target sector/cell, formation, cohesion radius.
- Team morale, suppression, fatigue, casualties.
- Team inventory: ammo, food, medical, construction, command, energy, shells, repair, carried cargo.
- Current reason string.

Team tasking families:

- Scout: find, observe, mark suspicion/contact, survive.
- Dig/Fortify: build only after confirmed or held contact.
- Assault: cross no man's land, breach, seize, clear cover.
- Supply/Logistics: carry supplies from base/depot to teams, caches, emplacements, and aid points.
- Command/Observer: coordinate sectors, spot artillery, improve cohesion.
- Emplacement Crew: build/man MGs, mortars, guns, siege positions once eligible.

Leaders influence morale, order quality, cohesion, and response speed. Leader loss should degrade the team rather than instantly destroying it.

## 13. Fortifications, Cover States, Crouch/Head-Up Behavior

Terrain cover:

- Exposed: open ground, road, mud, cleared field. High spotting and hit risk.
- Half cover: crater lip, rubble, low wall, sandbag scrape, shallow foxhole, obstacle edge. Moderate protection.
- Full cover: trench, reinforced trench, bunker, dugout entrance, heavy ruin, gun pit. Strong direct-fire protection.

Posture:

- Head-up/firing: can see, shoot, signal, observe, and operate weapons; more exposed.
- Crouched/hunkered: better survival, poor spotting/fire, useful for recovery and suppression decay.
- Pinned: minimal action, high survival only if cover exists.
- Braced/working: digging, loading, repairing, building, manning a weapon; vulnerable but productive.
- Dug-in: unit benefits from prepared position and cover source.

Fortifications:

- Scrape: fast emergency half-cover after contact.
- Foxhole: one-cell stronger half-cover, built after held contact.
- Fire Trench: full-cover firing cell.
- Communication Trench: full-cover movement/resupply link.
- Reinforced Trench: stronger trench with revetment/concrete/plates.
- Sandbag Wall / Parapet: fast half-cover.
- Barbed Wire / Scrap Wire: slows movement and supports MG kill zones.
- Rubble Barricade: converted obstacle, half-cover and line-of-sight blocker.
- Dugout: recovery/protection cell, poor firing value.
- Observation Post / Listening Post: spotting and early warning.
- Aid Post: wounded stabilization.
- Ammunition Niche / Forward Cache: local supply buffer with risk.
- Command Post: cohesion, order, and rally support.
- Aether Relay Shrine: Tier III command/energy node with instability risk.

Hard rule: no unit builds or digs before confirmed/held contact. Suspected contact allows observation and caution, not construction.

## 14. Emplacements And Artillery/MG Systems

Emplacements require eligible contact-born sectors, construction goods, crew, supply, and usually spotting/command.

Core emplacements:

- Light Machine-Gun Nest: Field Engineer/Sapper builds; MG Crew mans; consumes ammo; suppresses exposed/head-up units.
- Heavy Machine-Gun Bunker: stronger full-cover MG position; costs concrete/revetments, machine support, ammo.
- Trench Mortar Pit: indirect short fire against cover and clusters; needs shell supply and sighting.
- Field Gun Pit: heavy sector fire; needs shells, gun crew, observer/signaller, prepared position.
- Siege Gun Platform: Tier V base-destruction support; needs siege shells, bombardment cores, targeting, energy, command, protection.
- Signal/Spotter Post: improves artillery accuracy and response.
- Aether Searchlight: reveals hidden/crouched units; costs energy and becomes a target.
- Clockwork Loader Station: improves mortar/gun/siege reload; costs repair and energy.
- Warded Shell Cache: safer volatile shell storage; costs wards/bone-salt.
- Pressure Projector Pit: short-range bunker/trench denial; costs fuel/energy/reagents and carries friendly-risk.

Artillery should not be a global damage button. It should depend on supply, observer quality, command links, position, reload, target memory, and variance.

## 15. Supply/Logistics Teams And Frontline Consumption

Supply teams are real map actors.

Loop:

1. Receive request from team, sector, cache, aid post, command post, or emplacement.
2. Load cargo at base/depot.
3. Route through scouted, controlled, lower-threat cells.
4. Prefer trenches and communication trenches once built.
5. Avoid active contact unless emergency priority.
6. Deliver goods, then return, reroute, crouch, drop cargo, or retreat.

Frontline consumption:

- Ammo: firing, MG suppression, assault staying power.
- Food: scout range, morale, fatigue recovery, long build jobs, team cohesion.
- Medical: wounded stabilization and casualty reduction.
- Construction: scrapes, foxholes, trenches, wire, repairs, emplacements.
- Command: coordination, observer calls, response speed, bombardment control.
- Energy: magic teams, searchlights, wards, robotics, late siege.
- Machine support/repair: MGs, guns, robotics, loaders, emplacements.
- Shells/bombardment: mortars, field guns, siege guns, base breach.

Shortages should alter behavior:

- Low ammo: avoid assault, retreat sooner, conserve fire.
- Low food: scout less, dig slower, morale recovers poorly.
- Low construction: win contact but fail to consolidate.
- Low medical: more permanent losses.
- Low machine support: emplacements jam or go idle.
- Low Essence/energy: magic/TWB systems degrade; farms may starve the whole factory.

## 17. Organic War Behavior Implications

Organic-feeling war should come from team intent, local memory, cover, supply, posture, and bounded randomness.

Recommended AI architecture:

- War blackboard: known cells, suspected/confirmed contact, obstacles, owned trenches, supply requests, danger, route safety.
- Influence maps: friendly/enemy presence, danger, cover value, supply reach, visibility age, movement cost, contact recency.
- Utility decisions: scout target, hold/retreat, posture, dig target after contact, supply target, assault timing.
- Behavior states: forming, moving, scouting, listening, contact, suppressed, assaulting, retreating, regrouping, supplying, digging after contact, manning emplacement.
- Tactical position scoring: choose nearby cells by cover, line of sight, danger, route, and team job.

Organic rules:

- Scouts divide sectors and avoid duplicating paths.
- Teams do not always charge; they observe, crouch, retreat, regroup, call support, or wait for suppression.
- Assault teams should prefer attacking after enemy suppression, supply readiness, or command support.
- Dig-In teams wait for confirmed/held contact before building.
- Supply teams avoid danger unless emergency orders override.
- Seeded randomness changes texture, not logic. It should influence route preference, hesitation, cover choice, contact friction, and pacing, not make teams stupid.

Trench-network evolution system:

1. Scout contact: scouts observe, crouch, hide, mark suspicion, or retreat. No construction yet.
2. Confirmed contact: fighting creates contact markers and local danger memory.
3. Won or held contact: survivors and Dig-In teams create scrapes or foxholes on the cells that actually mattered.
4. Repeated/hot contact: foxholes upgrade into stronger positions because the sector keeps drawing fire.
5. Lateral connection: nearby foxholes, craters, and held positions are connected into short trench runs.
6. Strongpoint formation: MG pits, aid posts, observation posts, wire, caches, and command posts attach to durable trench runs.
7. Communication trenching: supply/dig teams begin trench links from the strongpoint back toward safer held ground and eventually toward base or depot.
8. Supply trench validation: if supply teams repeatedly reroute, stall, or take losses on a route, the AI marks that route for duckboards, communication trench, cache, or alternate path work.
9. Network maintenance: damaged, captured, abandoned, or isolated trench segments remain as terrain and can be reused, repaired, contested, or cut off.

The "front line" is therefore a derived network, not a drawn boundary. It is the shape made by contact-born foxholes, connected trenches, supply routes, held strongpoints, and active team pressure.

Recommended trench-network data:

- `ContactAge` and `ContactHeat`: how often fighting happened here.
- `HeldTicks`: how long a faction has held or survived in the cell/sector.
- `TrenchStage`: none, scrape, foxhole, fire trench, reinforced trench, communication trench.
- `TrenchOwner` and `LastContestedTick`.
- `NetworkId`: connected trench/supply network.
- `SupplyReach`: whether food/ammo/medical/construction can reach the position.
- `BaseLinkScore`: how close this network is to a safe path back to base/depot.
- `BuildIntent`: hold, lateral connect, dig back, reinforce, emplacement, cache, aid, command.

## 20. Risks, Open Questions, And Sequencing Notes

Risks:

- Too many items early can make the game feel like paperwork rather than strategy.
- Too few items makes production feel shallow and free.
- Essence can become over-centralized because it powers both food and magic.
- Steel can choke every factory/military path if diagnostics are weak.
- Team abstraction can hide important sub-unit behavior if inspectors are poor.
- Supply teams can become tedious if over-managed or fake if they teleport goods.
- No-build-before-contact makes early war quieter, so scouting/contact UI must be excellent.
- Artillery and MGs can dominate if counters, supply limits, and posture are not tuned.
- Magic and robotics can erase WW1 logistics if upkeep and instability are too soft.

Open questions:

- Does emergency scrape require confirmed contact or held contact?
- Are base-adjacent defenses also blocked before contact, or is there a special base exception?
- Should food spoilage exist in the first playable slice? Recommendation: no.
- Should Iron Ore exist from the start, or should Heavy Scrap carry early metal? Recommendation: start with Heavy Scrap.
- Should teams be fixed templates or player-designed? Recommendation: fixed templates first.
- How many teams can run before the `1000 x 600` war map becomes a simulation or rendering problem?
- Should NPC enemies use identical team templates or simplified mirrored logic? Recommendation: same concepts, simpler NPC supply curves for phase 1.

Sequencing note:

Do not implement the entire catalog at once. Prove the loop with a few resources, three allocation destinations, four team families, contact-born digging, and one emplacement. Then broaden the roster. A prototype should acquire complexity like a gentleman acquiring opinions: slowly, and only after evidence.
