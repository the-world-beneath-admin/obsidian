# Kickstarter Goal and Budget

## Exact Recommendation

# Set the Kickstarter base goal at **$150,000**.

That is the number I would use.

Not $75,000. Not $100,000. Not $200,000 as the base.

**$150,000** is the best balance between:

- being big enough to actually give you a year of runway,
- being realistic enough to explain to backers,
- surviving Kickstarter/payment fees,
- allowing tax reserve,
- giving you emergency operating money,
- and not making the base goal look like an unfocused empire-building ask.

## Why $150,000

You said your personal survival cost is about **$3,000 per month**.

That means:

| Need | Amount |
|---|---:|
| 12 months founder survival runway | $36,000 |
| Emergency/equipment/side operating reserve | $30,000 |
| AI/cloud/tools/community stack | $12,000 |
| Legal/IP/creator-license setup | $10,000 |
| Contractor/art/audio/code reserve | $6,000 |
| Campaign/community marketing | $3,000 |
| Admin/accounting/distribution/platform setup | $3,000 |
| Working buffer | $5,000 |
| **Needed after fees and reserves** | **$105,000** |

A $150,000 campaign does not leave you with $150,000 to spend. You need to plan around deductions.

| Deduction / Reserve | Estimate |
|---|---:|
| Kickstarter + payment processing reserve | $15,000 |
| Tax/CPA reserve | $30,000 |
| **Usable working budget** | **$105,000** |

This is why the base goal should be $150,000.

## Why Not $100,000

A $100,000 goal sounds easier to hit, but it is probably too low for what you are promising.

After fees and a tax reserve, it could leave you with roughly $65,000 to $75,000 of usable working room. That is not enough for:

- 12 months of survival,
- equipment failures,
- legal setup,
- AI/cloud costs,
- the Creator Kit,
- the canon archive,
- the AI coordinator,
- community launch,
- and Trenchworks delivery.

You would likely end up underfunded immediately.

## Why Not $200,000 As the Base Goal

$200,000 may be useful as a stretch goal level, but I would not make it the base goal unless you already have a very strong pre-launch audience.

A high base goal can scare off backers if the campaign looks too early.

Use this structure instead:

- **$150,000 = Phase One is funded.**
- **$175,000+ = World Key minigames begin unlocking.**
- **$200,000+ = first major expansion package: World Key + book expansion.**

That gives backers a reachable first target and a reason to push beyond it.

## The Campaign Funding Sentence

Use this line on the Kickstarter page:

> **The $150,000 goal funds one year of founder runway, Trenchworks delivery, the Creator Kit, the first living canon archive, the AI canon coordinator prototype, legal setup for creator participation, and the community launch system that lets The World Beneath begin growing beyond one person.**

## What Backers Are Actually Funding

Backers are not buying equity. They are not investing in the company. They are helping fund Phase One.

Phase One should be defined as:

1. **Garden is already free before launch** - the free proof-of-world web app.
2. **Trenchworks** - first paid game/code reward.
3. **Book and audiobook access** - story foundation.
4. **Creator Kit v1** - rules, assets, and creator-start materials.
5. **Living Canon Archive v1** - the organized memory of the world.
6. **AI Canon Coordinator Prototype** - helps creators check ideas against the canon.
7. **Community Hub** - Discord/community setup for creators and fans.
8. **Legal/IP groundwork** - creator license, contractor agreements, and rules.
9. **Founder runway** - one year to build instead of constantly stopping to survive.

## Internal Funding Thresholds

| Amount Raised | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Under $150,000 | Kickstarter fails because base goal is not met. |
| $150,000 | Phase One funded. Do not add major new promises. |
| $175,000 | First World Key minigame stretch goal becomes safe. |
| $200,000 | First major expansion package becomes safe. |
| $250,000 | Better creator tools and multiple World Keys become possible. |
| $300,000+ | More books/audiobooks and stronger archive tooling become realistic. |

## The Most Important Budget Rule

Do not spend like you raised the gross number.

Spend like you raised the post-fee, post-reserve number.

For a $150,000 campaign, act like the real working budget is about **$105,000** until your accountant tells you otherwise.
