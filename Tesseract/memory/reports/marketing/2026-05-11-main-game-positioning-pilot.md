# Marketing Report - 2026-05-11 - Main Game Positioning Pilot

## Task

Create a focused main-game positioning recommendation for **The World Beneath** that can guide later SEO, Steam/store copy, creator outreach, and website messaging.

## Research Question

What should the first public-facing positioning lane for The World Beneath main game be: shared-world RPG, dungeon runs, creature growth, crafting/progression, community-first development, or a combined pitch?

## Result

Approved by user on 2026-05-11: lead with the concrete RPG loop first, then frame it as a shared-world RPG.

Best current lane:

> A shared-world RPG where dungeon runs feed creature growth, crafting, inventory progression, and a larger world shaped by player societies.

This keeps the game understandable to a cold player before asking them to care about the broader ecosystem.

## Positioning Lanes Considered

### Lane 1 - Shared-World RPG First

Pitch shape:

> The World Beneath is a shared-world RPG where players help shape a hidden world through dungeons, creatures, crafting, societies, and long-run progression.

Strengths:

- Aligns with current public copy.
- Helps distinguish the project from isolated dungeon crawlers or creature battlers.
- Leaves room for societies, trade, territory pressure, World Keys, and community/account identity.

Weaknesses:

- "Shared-world RPG" may be too abstract for cold players.
- It can sound like future ambition instead of a playable loop.
- It risks inviting MMO-scale expectations too early.

Verdict:

- Useful as the frame, but too abstract to be the first hook by itself.

### Lane 2 - Dungeon Runs First

Pitch shape:

> Run dungeons, claim rewards, grow stronger creatures, and craft your way deeper into The World Beneath.

Strengths:

- Immediately explains what the player does.
- Supports Steam/search language around RPG, dungeon crawler, loot, progression, and fantasy.
- Matches the main-game architecture around dungeon, combat, reward, inventory, crafting, and snapshot systems.

Weaknesses:

- On its own, it can sound generic.
- It underplays the creature and shared-world differentiators.
- It may compete directly with dungeon crawlers that have clearer combat visuals and established expectations.

Verdict:

- Strongest cold-player entry point, but it needs creature growth and shared-world context nearby.

### Lane 3 - Creature Growth First

Pitch shape:

> Grow strange creatures through dungeon rewards, crafting choices, affinities, and long-run progression.

Strengths:

- Gives the game a more distinctive emotional hook.
- Connects to Siralim-like creature/party-building language without fully copying monster-catching positioning.
- Supports future content about roles, affinities, tiers, upgrades, and team choices.

Weaknesses:

- "Creature collector" could overpromise if collection is not the first public center.
- It may obscure the dungeon/crafting spine.
- It needs visual proof and system clarity before becoming the main public promise.

Verdict:

- Excellent secondary hook and backup lane, but probably not the first headline until creature identity is visually strong.

### Lane 4 - Crafting / Progression First

Pitch shape:

> Turn dungeon rewards into crafting paths, creature upgrades, inventory decisions, and deeper progression.

Strengths:

- Ties directly to the main-game architecture: Will, materials, catalysts, recipes, crafting, ApplyV2, inventory, reward claims.
- Good for devlogs and SEO content about systems.
- Appeals to players who like long-run optimization.

Weaknesses:

- Too systems-heavy for first-contact marketing.
- Can sound like a feature list instead of a fantasy.
- Needs the dungeon and creature fantasy to make it feel less mechanical.

Verdict:

- Strong supporting pillar, not the lead.

### Lane 5 - Community-First Development

Pitch shape:

> Join the community while The World Beneath takes shape, with account identity, lore, forums, Discord, and future shared-world systems.

Strengths:

- Honest about the current public state.
- Useful for Discord/account/forum conversion.
- Fits the public emphasis on community, books/audio, progress, and world formation.

Weaknesses:

- Too soft as a game pitch.
- Risks sounding like a lore/community project rather than a game.
- Does not answer "what do I do?" fast enough.

Verdict:

- Useful call-to-action layer, not the main positioning lane.

## Recommended Primary Lane

Decision Candidate - Use a combined **concrete loop first, shared-world frame second** lane.

Recommended structure:

1. Start with the concrete actions: dungeon runs, creature growth, crafting, inventory/progression.
2. Then explain the larger frame: shared-world RPG, societies, trade, territory pressure, community, account identity.
3. Treat lore/books/audio as gateways into the world, not replacements for the game pitch.

Recommended working one-liner:

> The World Beneath is a shared-world RPG where dungeon runs feed creature growth, crafting, inventory progression, and a larger hidden world shaped by player societies.

Shorter working variant:

> Run dungeons, grow strange creatures, craft stronger paths forward, and help shape a shared world beneath the ordinary one.

## Recommended Backup Lane

Decision Candidate - Use **creature-growth dungeon RPG** as the backup lane for Steam/store experiments.

Backup working line:

> A creature-growth dungeon RPG about dungeon runs, crafting choices, and long-run progression inside a hidden shared world.

Why this backup matters:

- It is more concrete than "shared-world RPG."
- It may perform better with cold players who search for creature collector, dungeon crawler, monster collector, party-based RPG, or progression RPG.
- It avoids leading with society/territory systems before those are proven as a public hook.

## Signals

- Signal - Public main-game copy frames the project around dungeon runs, creature growth, crafting, inventory, societies, trade, territory pressure, and community/account identity.
- Signal - Local main-game architecture supports dungeon/combat/reward/inventory/crafting/apply/snapshot/UI as the spine.
- Signal - Main-game bootstrap docs identify Phase 3 content expansion as the current priority, with content pools, dungeon content, and progression/economy as start areas.
- Signal - Adjacent Steam references combine broad genre tags with concrete mechanic/fantasy terms rather than relying on abstract world language alone.
- Signal - Current memory already warns that "shared-world RPG" should not stand alone without concrete verbs and systems nearby.

## Hypotheses

- Hypothesis - Cold players will understand "dungeon runs + creature growth + crafting" faster than "shared-world RPG."
- Hypothesis - "Shared-world RPG" becomes useful after the concrete loop is established because it differentiates the project from ordinary dungeon crawlers.
- Hypothesis - "Creature-growth dungeon RPG" may be a stronger Steam/store test phrase than "shared-world RPG" if creature systems become visually clear.
- Hypothesis - Community/account/lore messaging should be a conversion layer after the game promise, not the first line of the pitch.

## Decision Candidates

- Decision Candidate - Approve "concrete loop first, shared-world frame second" as the main-game positioning rule.
- Decision Candidate - Use this working one-liner for near-term drafts: "The World Beneath is a shared-world RPG where dungeon runs feed creature growth, crafting, inventory progression, and a larger hidden world shaped by player societies."
- Decision Candidate - Use "creature-growth dungeon RPG" as the first backup lane for store/capsule/click tests.
- Decision Candidate - Keep "MMO", "idle", and "automation" out of primary main-game positioning until explicitly approved.

## Risks

- Risk - "Shared-world RPG" may be unclear without immediate verbs.
- Risk - "Creature collector" may overpromise if collecting is not the central first public experience.
- Risk - Societies, trade, territory pressure, and larger world systems may sound live before the build supports them.
- Risk - Leaning too hard into lore/books/audio can make the project sound like a story world with a game later.
- Risk - Leaning too hard into mechanics can flatten the unique hidden-world identity.

## Memory-Worthy Notes

- Warning - Do not lead main-game marketing with Glassroot farming, alchemy, idle, cozy, or automation language.
- Warning - Use "shared-world RPG" as a frame, not a standalone cold hook.
- Decision Candidate - Main-game positioning should lead with dungeon runs, creature growth, crafting, and progression, then widen into shared-world/community systems.
